# What Forward Deployed Engineers do

## Short definition

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) is a software engineer who works closely with a customer or operational team to build and deploy software for that team's real problems. The role combines engineering with customer discovery, system integration, and responsibility for whether a solution works in practice.

The exact job varies by employer. Some FDE roles focus on AI applications; others involve data platforms, enterprise software, hardware, or specialized products.

## Typical work cycle

1. **Understand the workflow.** Talk with users and technical teams to learn what they do, what is difficult, and what constraints matter.
2. **Define the problem.** Turn an unclear request into requirements, success measures, and a realistic project scope.
3. **Design the solution.** Decide how the application, APIs, databases, models, and customer systems should work together.
4. **Build and integrate.** Write code, connect systems, test assumptions, and work through technical issues with the customer team.
5. **Evaluate.** Check accuracy, reliability, security, performance, and whether the solution improves the intended workflow.
6. **Deploy and support adoption.** Help move the system into production, document it, train or support users, and improve it based on feedback.
7. **Reuse what was learned.** Turn repeated solutions and lessons into better tools, patterns, and product feedback.

Not every posting lists every step, but end-to-end delivery is a recurring theme in the examples reviewed.

## How it differs from a traditional software role

| Traditional product engineering focus | Forward deployed engineering focus |
|---|---|
| Build software for a product's broader user base | Build or adapt software around a specific customer's workflow |
| Requirements may come through product planning | Engineers often help discover and define requirements directly with users |
| Work is usually organized around a product roadmap | Work may involve customer timelines, infrastructure, and changing constraints |
| Success includes product quality and adoption | Success also includes deployment outcomes and measurable customer impact |

These are broad tendencies, not strict boundaries. Product engineers also work with users, and FDEs still need strong software engineering fundamentals.

## What the job is not

- It is not only giving presentations or recommending technology.
- It is not just making a quick demo that never reaches production.
- It is not limited to prompt writing or using AI tools.
- It is not one standardized job description across every company.

## What the postings show

Google's Summer 2027 internship describes building AI applications, connecting them to customer infrastructure, and creating evaluation and observability pipelines. OpenAI's FDE postings emphasize discovery, architecture, implementation, evaluation, production rollout, and customer adoption. Palantir's Mixed Reality posting describes close collaboration with customers, operators, designers, and developers.

**Bottom line:** FDEs need to understand people and workflows, but their core contribution is building and delivering software that works in the customer's environment.
